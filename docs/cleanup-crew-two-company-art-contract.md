# Cleanup Crew: two-company art contract

2026-10-12. User direction: continue solidifying art; the cleaning company and the client office are different companies. The user explicitly accepts a colour change as enough separation. Keep the loved office-lived-bullpen look. No second modelling style, alternate furniture kit or office redesign is required.

## One game, two palettes

Shared execution: readable stylized volumes, restrained bevels, deliberate material contrast, moderate detail, credible object function and Roblox avatar scale. Existing office furniture/clutter stays. Offline concept lighting is a reference, not an engine specification.

## Ownership map

- Cleaning company: issued vacuum/tools, crew trucks, depot, company vest/badge, dispatch paperwork, guide/equipment/settings/results UI. Existing teal paperwork kit stays.
- Client office: office partitions, doors, furniture, office bins, lockers, carpets, wall notices and its own stationery. Recolour former teal areas to slate/blue-grey; retain cream lockers, warm oak and clutter.
- Cargo belongs to the visited workplace unless it is a issued bag/tool. Do not stamp crew branding onto every salvage prop. Dust bags/issued bags may visibly belong to the cleaning crew.
- Harlow is associated with the client workplace; do not dress him in the crew uniform or repeat crew branding. Native avatar proportion and animation proof remains required.

## Recolour instructions for asset/layout owner

Keep the bullpen/hallway forms and placements. Recolour teal partition/wall paint, upholstered chairs, moulded office bins and local folders to subdued slate/blue-grey. Carpet becomes neutral charcoal-grey. Preserve wood, cream metal, graphite monitors/keyboards, cork, white paperwork, incidental mugs and warm fluorescent lights. No new geometry is requested by this contract. Truck/vacuum proportions are separate gameplay/art decisions, not forced to match office furniture.

Default company equipment remains teal/ivory/amber. Keep current candidate truck work; this clarification removes the earlier rationale for switching trucks to match the office. Bulky-B versus Pro-A stays a tool silhouette/hand-contact choice, not an office palette choice. Do not overwrite the other asset agent's builds from this document.

## UI and future cosmetics

Company-owned menus use the existing UI kit; world client notices use client colours. Do not recolour the entire shift HUD to match the current room. Hover/focus/selected/disabled and warnings keep their established readable semantics.

Player personalization belongs primarily to crew-owned tools, vest/badges, clipboard and truck livery. Prepare paint/trim/decal material regions and optional cosmetic sockets in those assets. Client offices may vary their paint/furnishings by contract in future without changing the crew's identity. Those environment variants are content direction, not a proposed player purchase of the client's furniture. A personal locker display, if later implemented, belongs in the crew depot.

Cosmetic sound variants must preserve gameplay noise cues and actual detection rules. Custom skins should remain recognizable at gameplay scale. No shop, ownership system, purchase, asset upload or place publish is added by this art contract; public demand has not yet been tested.

## Acceptance slice

One existing bullpen bay recoloured in Studio, with a native avatar, company vest/reference and teal vacuum. Inspect normal and night lighting, desktop and phone camera, grip scale, route width, and mess versus dressing. Success: equipment reads as brought into the space, while assets still share one game's stylization. Only palette changes are needed for the first comparison. No global office layout rebuild or company UI redesign.
