1. Cleaning without holding the click

Today: you hold the left mouse button (R2 on a gamepad, the vacuum button on a phone) the whole time you clean. While you hold it, you also move the mouse to steer the ring's icon. You can walk at 75% speed while cleaning.

Phones have it worst: one thumb is on the move stick and the other holds the vacuum button. That leaves no finger free to steer the ring.

2. A stat for the ring

Today: every employee has 5 stats shown as 1–5 icons: Speed, Stamina, Strength, Suction and Quiet. Every employee adds up to 15. One bar plays like the base game, and each bar above it helps a little (for example, Suction is 4% faster cleaning per bar).

What a ring stat would change: my pick is the opening's width. It's easy to see and fair, and with 1 bar it plays like today, so solo play never gets harder. Other ideas were a slower ring or fewer rings per pile. Both are harder to feel, and fewer rings means less game.

3. Longer nights in Night Shift

Today: nights are 3:00 in week 1, 5:00 in week 2, 7:00 in week 3, and 8:30 from week 4 on. Goals grow each night: for one player, night 1 is 190 Gloom and night 5 is 320. Quick shift (8:30) doesn't change with any of this.

"Pace" means the Gloom you must clean per minute to make the goal. Lower pace = less clock stress. Today the hardest pace is at the end of week 1 (107 a minute).

Pace is for one player, from the first to the last night of each week. Goals stay as they are. More players get bigger goals, so the pace stays about the same.

For longer nights: the clock stops being the main stress. You spend more time in the building with the manager walking around, so the scare comes from him, not from a timer. Kids who play slower can still make it.

Against: runs get longer. Week 1 goes from about 15 minutes of play to about 20. With T1, week 4 nights reach 11:30, which is long for a kid. With too much spare time, goals can feel too easy for 2+ players who split up. After launch, we can watch the analytics (which night runs end on, and why) to tune it.
