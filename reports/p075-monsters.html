What makes a stalker scary (what I took from other games)

  • A tell before the danger. Doors' Rush flickers the lights and rumbles before it comes. Piggy and Dandy's World play a sound that gets louder as the monster nears. Kids learn the tell, then dread it.
  • Watching must cost you something. Lethal Company's Coil-head (a freeze-when-seen monster) is scary because you have to look away to work. Right now our camera can stare at Bramble while your character keeps cleaning, so nothing is lost by watching.
  • He's never where you left him. Weeping-angel monsters work because every blink or blackout moves them. The scare is the gap: "he was over there a second ago".
  • Silence, then loud. Granny and Apeirophobia stay quiet until the monster is close. A sudden change is scarier than a steady sound.
  • Third person needs help to feel close. First-person games get "he's right in my face" for free. In third person we have to pull the camera in for that moment, briefly.
  • Set pieces. Doors' Seek hallway and Halt corridor are short, scripted runs everyone remembers. One per shift is enough.

Part 1: Bramble, scarier

Today: patrol 16, chase 27 (a sprint is 23), freezes while a crew member's camera looks within 35° of him. A hit takes one coat. He has no footsteps yet.

B1

Busy hands, busy eyes

rulessmall buildfixes "you can just clean while he's staring"
How it plays
While your vacuum is on, you don't count as watching Bramble. To freeze him you must stop cleaning and look. So one kid watches while another cleans, or you clean in short bursts and keep checking.
Why it's scary
Every second of cleaning is a second he can move. It's the Coil-head rule: the job and the watching fight each other.
Look-to-freeze
Makes the rule matter. Watching is still the only way to stop him.
Kid safety
Nothing visual. The tip can say: "You can't watch Bramble and clean at the same time!"
Cost
Small. The watch report already goes to the server; it skips players with the vacuum on.
Vacuuming stops you from freezing Bramble?
B2

The lights blink

ruleslookmedium build
How it plays
Every 20 to 40 seconds, the lights where he stands buzz for 1 second, then go dark for half a second. In the dark nobody can see him, so he jumps up to 10 studs closer. When the lights come back, he's closer and still.
Why it's scary
The buzz is a tell you start to dread. You keep watching and he still gets closer.
Look-to-freeze
It's the weeping-angel "blink". Watching still works, just not during the blink.
Kid safety
One blink, never flashing. At least 15 seconds between blinks. Never fully black.
Cost
Medium. Needs the School's room lights grouped by room (a small map tag pass if they aren't yet).
Add the light blink that lets him jump closer?
B3

Quiet as a bat, then loud and quick

rulessoundsmall buildyour ask
How it plays
Far away he makes no sound at all. Within about 18 studs his steps suddenly turn on: loud, quick, soft-foam thumps with a feather rustle, faster as he gets closer. They stop dead whenever he's frozen. The heartbeat and dark edges we already use for Mr. Harlow come on with them.
Why it's scary
Silence lets him sneak up. The sudden switch to loud and quick says "he's right here, turn around".
Look-to-freeze
When the steps stop, you know someone is watching him. When they start again, nobody is.
Kid safety
Soft foam thumps, not stomps or screams. Volume kept under the chase music.
Cost
Small. A Bramble footstep set plus a distance switch, and the dread effects turned on for him.
Silent far away, loud quick steps inside about 18 studs?
B4

The perch

ruleslayoutmedium build
How it plays
If nobody has seen him for 15 seconds and he's far away, he moves to a high spot near the crew that no camera can see: on top of the lockers, the trophy case, a basketball hoop, the stage. You walk in and he's up there, looking down. Look away and you hear one thump as he drops to the floor.
Why it's scary
He's never where you left him, and you start checking up high in every room. It also fits an owl.
Look-to-freeze
On a perch he freezes like anywhere else. He only moves to a perch where nobody is looking.
Kid safety
A reveal, not a jumpscare: no sound sting when you spot him.
Cost
Medium. Perch spots tagged by the School generator, a perch pose and a drop, and an "only where unseen" move.
Let him move to high perches when nobody sees him?
B5

"He's right here" camera

looksmall to medium buildthird-person answer to first-person
How it plays
The first time each life that he gets within 6 studs of you, your camera pulls in close over your shoulder for about half a second and the view narrows, so he fills the screen. His owl head turns all the way round to face you. Then the camera goes back.
Why it's scary
It gives a first-person "in my face" moment without making the game first person.
Look-to-freeze
The pull-in points your camera at him, so it also freezes him. It's a scare and a save at once.
Kid safety
Short, no scream, no face close-up beyond his stitched owl look. Turned off by the reduced-motion setting.
Cost
Small to medium. Uses the Camera feature; needs a play-check on phones.
Add the quick close-camera moment?
B6

The long locker hall

layoutrulesbigger build
How it plays
Once a shift, when a player is in the main locker hall, the lights at the far end go out one bank at a time, coming toward you. Each time a bank goes dark, the lockers in it rattle and Bramble shows up in the next lit bank, closer. Get out of the hall, or keep looking at him until the lights come back.
Why it's scary
A short set piece kids will talk about, like the hallway runs in Doors.
Look-to-freeze
He can only move through dark banks. Lit and watched, he's stuck.
Kid safety
About 10 seconds, rattles not bangs, and the hall always has a lit way out.
Cost
Bigger. Needs a tagged hall, light banks and a scripted event. Better after launch.
Plan the locker-hall set piece (after launch)?

Part 2: a second monster for any map

One rule I kept for all of these: no employee turns evil. That is what makes Dandy's World what it is (its own toons twisted into monsters). Ours come from the building's leftovers and the company's old things, which already fits our lore: buildings grow trouble from mess and forgotten stuff. Each would join a shift through the roster plan from 11-10: the map's main monster starts the shift, the second one shows up later.

M1

Big Dust Bunny

my pickmedium build
Hook
Leave the mess, and the Dust Bunny gets bigger.
How it hunts
It hops from the dirtiest room to the next dirtiest. The more Gloom left on the map, the bigger, faster and wider-reaching it gets. A touch takes a coat.
Counterplay
Cleaning shrinks it. When it's small, vacuum it straight on and it pops into tiny bunnies that scatter (it comes back later). A heavy "thump, thump" hop and lint puffs tell you where it is.
With the others
With Harlow, it makes the quiet rooms busy. With Bramble, kids who only watch Bramble and never clean feed the bunny. It gives cleaning a way to fight back.
Look
A lumpy round ball of lint and Gloom, two long floppy ears, a stub tail. Grey with Gloom purple. No face. No clothes, so it never reads as Pippa.
Cost
Medium: a simple rig, a hop move, size tied to the Gloom count, the pop.
Keep Big Dust Bunny in the running?
M2

The Lookalike

medium build
Hook
One of your crew is made of Gloom.
How it hunts
It takes the shape of one of your real crewmates, solid Gloom all over. It acts like a friend: walks near you, stands by goo. If you get close while alone, it grabs and takes a coat.
Counterplay
Real crew have a name above their head; the Lookalike's tag is blank. Real friends wave back; it waves late. It's Gloom, so 3 seconds of vacuum pops it, and it forms again much later.
With the others
With Bramble it is nasty in a fun way: it can stand there "watching" him, but it doesn't freeze him. A kid who thinks a friend is watching gets caught.
Look
Crew-shaped, drippy edges, deep Gloom purple, smooth blank head, no face, blank lanyard. It copies whoever is in your lobby, not a fixed character, which is the opposite of a twisted toon.
Cost
Medium: copy a crew rig's shape, the tag rule, the vacuum pop.
Keep the Lookalike in the running?
M3

Unit-7, the old cleaning robot

bigger build
Hook
The old cleaning robot thinks you are the mess.
How it hunts
It rolls a route and goes toward noise and dropped things. When it sees you, its hose pulls you toward it and you have to run against the pull. If it gets you, it bags you: you pop out at the truck, one coat less, without what you carried.
Counterplay
It won't roll over a Wet Floor sign, so you can move signs to block halls. Unplug it at its dock to stop it for 30 seconds.
With the others
It's loud, so it hides Bramble's steps. With Harlow, its noise brings him over.
Look
A squat barrel on treads, a long ribbed hose arm, one amber work light, the company's old orange stripe.
Cost
Bigger: the pull, the sign blocking, the dock.
Keep Unit-7 in the running?
M4

Morry the Mop

medium build
Hook
The company's old mascot only dances while its song plays.
How it hunts
A mascot costume with a boombox. While its jingle plays, it dances toward the nearest player. When the song stops, anyone still moving is spotted and it zooms at them.
Counterplay
Stand still when the music stops. Vacuum the batteries out of its boombox for a long quiet break.
With the others
Good with Harlow. Not with Bramble: two "freeze" games at once is confusing, so never on the same shift. It is also close to the Roll Call idea from 11-10.
Look
A big mop head for a head with a little company cap, so there is no face. Faded orange and teal suit.
Cost
Medium: song timing, the movement check, the boombox.
Keep Morry the Mop in the running?
M5

Safety Sam, the cardboard cutout

small to medium build
Hook
The flat man from the old safety video. Turn him sideways and he's gone.
How it hunts
A life-size cardboard cutout. He's paper thin, so from the side you can't see him. He slides along walls and turns his thin edge toward anyone looking. A paper flap sound tells you he's moving. A touch slaps a "SAFETY FIRST" sticker on you and takes a coat.
Counterplay
Walk around to see his front. Your vacuum pulls him off the wall and folds him up for 10 seconds.
With the others
Good with Harlow. With Bramble it's two sight monsters, so I'd keep them apart.
Look
A printed thumbs-up worker with the face peeled off (no face). Faded print colours, bent corners.
Cost
Small to medium: a thin part already vanishes edge-on in the engine; the turn-to-watcher move is the work.
Keep Safety Sam in the running?
